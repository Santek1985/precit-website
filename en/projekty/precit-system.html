Where it came from

The trap of the commercial MES market

A typical production plant runs several, sometimes a dozen or so smaller systems. Each one with a separate license (and far from cheap), each from a different vendor, none of them talking directly to the others — or doing it very poorly. One system plans, another reports, a third watches the warehouse, a fourth handles schedules — and the operator still has to copy data between them by hand. Every change to any of those systems requires a request, a project plan, and a quote. The vendor needs time to evaluate and decide — which takes months, sometimes years.

At some point the client loses patience and starts looking around for a new, better system. But that's where the second problem begins: every next system bought off the shelf again doesn't match the company profile. Again the vendor's consultants say "this is the market standard, you have to adapt." Again, after a year, it turns out the operator is bypassing the system, management doesn't trust the reports, and every change still requires a request. Back to square one — with higher license costs and the same problem on the shop floor.

The main reason for that trap is simple: the people who design those systems don't understand production from the inside. They may have been on a shop floor at some point, may have had briefings from managers and engineers, may have read a hundred industry reports — but that's not the same as working for years at a machine and being responsible for the shift's result. Only from that level can you see where the office-based designer puts a form instead of a prompt, where a procedure leaves the operator alone at the moment of decision, and where the management report reflects an idea instead of the reality on the floor.

Monitor System was an earlier exercise and a workflow concept showcase — a working demonstrator that earned strong endorsement from operators and management. PrecIT System is the platform that grew out of that foundation and went further: full modularity, multi-tenant, on-premise, offline licensing, and a layered architecture that lets each client's scope be tailored individually.
Philosophy

The shop floor can only be learned by working on it

My edge isn't technology. There's no shortage of technology today. My edge is that I spent seven years on the production floor — first as an operator, then as a foreman responsible for an entire department. I know exactly the moment in a shift when an operator starts looking for ways to simplify their work. I know which field on the shift report nobody fills in honestly. I know what shift handover looks like when the next group is about to walk onto the floor and something from the previous shift hasn't been finished.

That knowledge lets me diagnose a shop floor quickly. In a few days of conversations and observation, I can point out the specific places where the current system leaves the operator without help and the management without reliable data. That then translates directly into PrecIT's configuration — from the company profile, through the structure of departments and lines, shift and brigade definitions, technological routings and identification cards, all the way to the operator's workflow at the machine and what the foreman, planner and executives actually need.

My way of working always involves a direct presence on the client's shop floor. I don't work solely from a production manager's briefing or from process documentation. I go onto the floor, I talk with operators at the machines, I observe the workflow during a shift. Otherwise the system will technically work — but it won't fit how people actually work. And then the operator will find a way to do things their own way anyway, and the system will become yet another headache.

What sets PrecIT System apart

Adapts to the plant — not the other way around

The most important principle of PrecIT's philosophy is: the system adapts to the plant, not the plant to the system. Sounds trivial, but in commercial MES systems you practically never see it. Everything that sets PrecIT apart from the competition comes from that one rule.

  • Modularity tailored to your needs — every module (Foreman, Process, Operator, Planner, Quality, Maintenance, HR, Safety, Notifications and others) is tailored to the specific client — only the functions they actually use, in a layout and workflow consistent with the plant's reality. The rest comes later without rebuilding.
  • Configurability without programming — structure of departments, lines, machines and roles; shift and brigade definitions; technological routings and alternative operation locations; batch number format (a configurable string of department code, date, shift and sequence); identification card layout (A5 standard, A6 compact, 100×70 mm label); custom card fields; quality control schedules — all of it set by the client's admin in the panel, without writing a line of code and without contacting the vendor.
  • Multi-tenant ready — one server instance handles multiple plants. tenant_id is present on every table except technical and live data. For holdings with several plants — one platform, separated data, shared executive reports.
  • On-premise Windows deployment — rolled out locally according to the client's IT department guidelines. No forced cloud, no telemetry to the vendor. Production data never leaves the client's infrastructure.
  • Open standards — Node.js, Express, React, PostgreSQL, WebSockets, Zod. Any senior developer can take this over and develop it further. With the purchase of full intellectual property the client receives the complete source code along with documentation and know-how transfer — in a form agreed with the client's IT department. Zero vendor lock-in, zero closed format.
Process module

Heart of the system — full part traceability

Most production plants today have some kind of part-counting system. Very few of them have real traceability of the part from raw material to customer shipment.

The Process module is the core of the PrecIT platform — the deepest and most extensive component of the system. The whole adaptation philosophy is realized inside it: every element of the traceability process is configured for the specific client plant — from the data structure to the way it's displayed, from validation rules to the look of printed documents accompanying the batch. The operator gets a workflow that guides them through successive production steps, and management gets the full history of every produced piece, available in seconds for audit or claim purposes.

The specific scope of the Process module — from execution recording, through technological routings, batch identification, BOM handling, all the way to full genealogy and claim handling — is always discussed individually for your production processes.

System architecture

Four layers with clearly separated responsibilities

PrecIT System is built in a layered architecture in which each layer has a clearly defined responsibility and import rules. This separation isn't just a convention — it's enforced by the structure of the code, which means a change in one module cannot cause a regression in another. That's a rarity in commercial MES, where often one badly designed change is enough to break two other modules.

Modules — functional modules
Foreman · Process · Operator · Planner · Quality · Maintenance · HR · Safety · Notifications · Procurement · Warehouse · Executive · Reports · TV Monitor · Costing · APS · IT. Every module registers its own endpoints and views, and every one can be disabled in config.json. Communication between modules happens exclusively through the event bus.
↓ imports
Shared — reusable domain components
EmployeeModal, PartModal, LineModal, MachineModal, services for fetching employees and part passports. They operate on entities shared by multiple modules. They adapt automatically to the active modules — turn Quality off and the "Quality" tab disappears from the employee modal, while the system keeps working.
↓ imports
Core — domain-neutral infrastructure
Auth, JWT, sessions, license validation, configuration, event bus, API client, PL/EN internationalization, UI widgets (Button, Modal, Toast, Table). Core knows nothing about an employee, a line, or a brigade — it's the domain-neutral foundation.
↓ reads
Data — PostgreSQL schemas
core.* (users, roles, sessions, permissions) · technical.* (lines, machines, departments, parts) · hr.* (employees, brigades, schedules, qualifications, medical exams, training) · live.* (notifications, production events) · traceability.* (batches, routings, BOM, cards). The foundation of the system — always present, never disabled.

The practical consequence of this architecture: a client disabling a module doesn't cause the system to crash. Functions disappear cleanly — tabs, sections, menu options simply don't render. Enable the module later — the functions come back as if they had never been off. I call this behavior graceful degradation, and it's built into the architecture, not bolted on as a workaround.

Technology stack

Technical decisions — no compromises for fashion

Technology choices in PrecIT come from concrete deployment needs. Nothing in here is added "because it's trendy" — every element has a justification on the operational side of the client and their IT department.

Backend: Node.js + Express + Knex.js (query builder) + PostgreSQL 18. JWT for authentication, Winston as the logger, Bull as the job queue, Zod for input validation.

Realtime: WebSockets (ws) with broadcasting of production events. An in-memory state layer with the option to plug in Redis as a drop-in replacement.

Frontend: React 18 with Babel standalone — no bundler, no webpack, no configuration. The client's IT engineer can open a JSX file, fix a line and refresh the browser. No toolchain complexity, no compiler to maintain.

Deployment: Windows on-premise, in line with the client's IT department guidelines. PostgreSQL local. No forced cloud, no dependency on external services, no telemetry.

Security: 135 permissions across 35 roles, dynamically modifiable in the database. tenant_id on every table apart from technical and live data. Detailed conditions, including licensing, agreed individually with each client.

Security and deployment

Your infrastructure, your data, your ownership

Production data is one of the most valuable assets of a plant. PrecIT System was designed so that this data never leaves the client's infrastructure. There's no forced cloud, no telemetry to the vendor. The system runs locally, on the client's servers, under the client's control — in line with the IT department's guidelines.

  • On-premise Windows deployment — installation on the client's servers, in line with the IT department's guidelines. Local PostgreSQL 18. Production data stays in the client's infrastructure.
  • Granular permission model — 135 permissions across 35 roles. Each role dynamically modifiable in the database. The operator sees their workstation and their shift. The team leader sees their brigade. Management sees their departments. The board sees the whole picture. Admin = superset of all modules with additional management features (employee transfers, change audits, dictionary editing).
  • Multi-tenant via tenant_id — every table with business data has a column identifying the plant. A single server instance can serve several plants of a holding — data separated, executive reports shared.
  • Change audit — every modification of key entities is logged with user ID, timestamp, and state before and after the change. Available to the administrator in the panel, and to the board in the Executive module.
  • Open source code — with a one-time IP purchase the client receives the complete source code, full technical documentation, and a description of the architecture and deployment procedures — in a form agreed with the client's IT department. Your IT team continues to develop the system independently — zero vendor lock-in.
  • NDA compliance — before every first conversation about processes and data we sign a confidentiality agreement. Your procedures and internal data don't leave this engagement.
