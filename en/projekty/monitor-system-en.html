The origin

The problem existed long before the first line of code

The systems available in factories didn't answer the basic operational questions: how many units were actually produced on this shift? Which workstation has been standing idle for 40 minutes and why? Will this order be finished before the shift ends? Data arrived late — entered at the end of the shift, summarised on a piece of paper, transferred to a spreadsheet. By the time it reached a manager, it was already history.

But the deeper problem wasn't the delay. It was that the system allowed procedures to be skipped. An operator could close a shift without entering a measurement result. A quality check that was supposed to happen every 2 000 parts happened when someone remembered. A problem that surfaced at the customer's facility started three shifts earlier — and nobody had a record of it.

I decided to change that. Monitor System was built as a response to real problems — not as a portfolio project, but as a tool I personally needed when I was running a production department.

The system was demonstrated to management and received very strong approval from both operators and production foremen. It was not deployed for organisational — not technical — reasons. It ran flawlessly in the test environment.
Capabilities

What the system does

Monitor System is a browser-based web application — no installation, runs on any device connected to the plant network. It covers the full production management cycle, from shift planning and live floor monitoring to quality enforcement and management reporting.

  • Production planning — shift plans per workstation with order numbers, product types, quantities and targets. The planner sets what needs to happen; the system tracks whether it does.
  • Operator Terminal — a dedicated interface at the machine. The operator sees their active plan item, live counter, shift plan status and three actions: complete item, perform measurement, report problem. No paper card. No end-of-shift data entry. Everything logged in real time.
  • Live foreman dashboard — real-time KPIs for the entire department: output vs. plan, OEE, downtime, availability — per workstation, per shift. The foreman sees what is happening right now, not what happened yesterday.
  • TV Mode — one click from the foreman panel launches a fullscreen kiosk for floor-mounted screens. Five production line tiles with live figures, automatic rotation every 30 seconds. Every person on the floor sees the current state of production without asking.
  • Quality Gate — mandatory measurement enforcement — when a defined interval is reached (e.g. every 2 000 parts), production is automatically halted. The operator must complete a full measurement cycle using a connected CMM device. Measurement data comes from the instrument, not from memory. A result outside tolerance triggers immediate escalation to the Shift Leader — a named person, visible on screen, with an extension number. The machine does not stop — non-conforming parts are separated automatically. But the next batch cannot begin until a decision has been made. This step cannot be skipped.
  • Production events log — every downtime event is registered with type, duration, workstation and who reported it. Changeovers, tooling changes, failures, calibrations — all timestamped and attributed. The foreman has a full picture at shift handover.
  • Shift schedule — a colour-coded monthly grid for all employees. Configurable shift start times per employee group: operators may start at 06:30, team leaders at 06:00 — the system handles both independently. Every module reads the correct shift boundaries automatically.
  • Reporting and archiving — automatic shift reports, daily summaries and monthly output charts. Full production history available for analysis. Excel export. The manager's morning report generates itself.
The key difference

Not a rigid, off-the-shelf solution

Standard MES systems cost hundreds of thousands of zlotys, take 12–18 months to implement and arrive with a fixed structure that the plant then has to adapt to. Every modification is a project, a timeline and an invoice. The result is a system that technically works — but that operators find ways around, because it was never designed for the reality of their shift.

Monitor System works differently. Every element — from the department and workstation structure, shift hours and user roles, through quality measurement schedules and escalation chains, to the visual design — is configured for the specific plant. The system adapts to the company, not the company to the system.

This is not a product you install and accept. It is a system built around your actual process — after I spend time on your floor understanding how work really flows, where the loopholes are and what the operator actually needs at the machine at 3am on the third shift.

What it proves

What this project demonstrates

Monitor System is proof that digitising a production department doesn't require a multi-hundred-thousand-euro ERP implementation or an 18-month rollout with a team of consultants. A working system — from operator logging to management KPIs — from concept to running prototype, built by one person who understands the shop floor from the inside.

The critical difference from anything available off the shelf: this system was designed by someone who personally operated every bypass it closes. I know what the operator does when the measurement form is optional. I know how foremen "correct" data at end of shift. I know which reports management actually reads and which ones go straight to the bin. Monitor System was built around that knowledge — not around a feature list from a product brochure.

Every plant is different. This project is a starting point — a demonstration of what's possible when a production system is built from the inside out. Every engagement starts with time spent on your floor before a single requirement is written down.
